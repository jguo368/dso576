Candidate Vision A:

What matters when making this decision?
- Prioritize counties and specialties with the greatest provider-capacity needs.
- Improve access to Medi-Cal providers in underserved/impacted areas.
- Use recruitment resources where they can have the greatest impact.
- Balance geographic needs with specialty-specific needs, each area has their own niche.

What could go badly?
- Resources could be directed toward counties that appear underserved in the data but do not have the greatest actual need.
- County level data could hide disparities within individual communities.
- Recruiting providers may not improve access if other barriers prevent patients from receiving care.
- Incomplete or outdated provider data could lead to poor prioritization.

What evidence would change the decision, and how would success be measured?
- Provider counts and specialties by county.
- Medi-Cal population/enrollment numbers by county.
- Provider availability relative to the population potentially needing care.

Success Measurement:
- Increased provider availability in targeted counties/areas.
- Improved provider to Medi-Cal population ratios.
- Reduced differences between relatively underserved and well-served areas.


Candidate Vision B:

What matters when making this decision?
- Identify populations experiencing the greatest barriers to accessing care.
- Determine whether language, telehealth, or transportation support best addresses the barrier.
- Allocate resources where they can meaningfully improve access.
- Reduce disparities among counties and population groups.

What could go badly?
- County-level patterns could hide differences among population groups.
- Demographic characteristics could be treated as evidence of an access barrier without sufficient support.
- Resources could be spread across too many interventions or areas to have a meaningful impact.

What evidence would change the decision, and how would success be measured?
Evidence:
- Medi-Cal enrollment and demographic information.
- Geographic distribution of patients and providers.
- Evidence of transportation barriers.
- Telehealth availability and utilization.

Success:
- Increased access/utilization among targeted populations and better language appropriate services.
- Greater appropriate use of telehealth and reduced transportation-related barriers.
- Reduced disparities between targeted and better served populations.
